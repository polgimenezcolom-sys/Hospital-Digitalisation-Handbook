# Funding the owner

**Produces:** the case for a paid IT post — with a number — put to the hospital board, and a written
answer.
**When:** raised before you fly, decided before the deployment's first year ends.
**Instrument:** [B-1](../4-Instruments/B1-Readiness.md) (`OWNER-5Y`); [B-3](../4-Instruments/B3-Mandate.md).

## Why this recipe exists

Every durable open-source hospital system that anyone has documented is sustained by a **permanent
institutional host with a recurrent budget and paid technical staff** — a ministry, a university, an
NGO, a company. Not one is sustained by volunteers. They die when the host is a *project*: a grant, a
cohort, a single champion.

A mission hospital has no ministry to fund a post. So the post has to be made, argued for, and paid
for by the hospital itself or by whoever funds it. This recipe is how to make that argument.

## What "the owner" means

A named person, employed by the hospital, whose job description includes *"this system works"*.
Part-time is fine at level 3; full-time at level 4. It is not a volunteer, not a student, not "the
technician will probably look after it".

## Steps

1. **Cost the current situation.** How many days a month does someone spend compiling returns by hand?
   How many lost folders, repeated tests, expired stock? Get real numbers from the six conversations
   ([Stakeholders](Stakeholders.md)). This is what the post buys back.
2. **Cost the post.** A local salary for a part-time or full-time IT technician. Ask the
   administrator; do not guess from Barcelona.
3. **Cost everything else recurrent.** Internet subscription. Hardware replacement — assume a
   workstation or an access point fails every year. Commercial support, if any. Electricity. Write it
   as a five-year figure.
4. **Compare four ways of paying for it.** Do-it-yourself open source with volunteers (what AUCOOP does
   now); open source with a paid local support partner; a commercial system; a hosted/managed
   open-source service. The companion thesis costs these for the model hospital. The evidence points
   to the second.
5. **Put it to the board in one page.** What it costs, what it saves, what happens if nobody is
   funded (the archive's answer: it goes dark within a year, and the next volunteers start again).
6. **Get an answer in writing.** Yes, with a source of funds and a start date. Or no — in which case
   go back to [B-2](../4-Instruments/B2-Level-Decision.md), because you are now deploying above what
   the hospital can carry.

## What goes wrong

**Assuming a funder will appear.** The 2017 Ethiopia thesis declared solar power a priority and left it
unfunded; the 2024 Lunsar design costed 5,866 € of equipment and nothing recurrent. Both are honest
documents that did not ask the question.

**Costing only year zero.** Equipment and flights are what volunteers see. The hospital sees the
internet bill every month, forever.

**Treating "no" as a failure.** A board that says *"we cannot fund an IT post this year"* has given
you the most useful answer of the trip. Deploy at a level that does not need one, and make the case
again next year with a year of evidence.
