# Securing the mandate

**Produces:** a medical director who has endorsed the project *to staff, in person*; a signed
agreement naming who owns what and who pays for what after you leave.
**When:** before configuration starts. Ideally before you fly.
**Instrument:** [B-3](../4-Instruments/B3-Mandate.md).

## Why permission is not enough

The director will say yes. Directors of under-resourced hospitals say yes to visiting engineers who
want to install things for free. That yes is real and you need it — and it changes nothing on the
wards, because a director's authority over how a senior nurse records her admissions is far weaker
than an organisational chart suggests, and rightly so.

What you need is not permission. It is an **instruction**, from the director, to the staff, that this
is how the hospital will work — plus released time, and a consequence for it not being used.

## Steps

1. **Meet the director alone, first.** Not with the board, not with the administrator. Ask what
   *they* want from this, in their words. Write it down verbatim.
2. **Ask the three uncomfortable questions**, in this order:
    - *Who will be responsible for this system in a year?* If the answer is a role that does not
      exist, that is your first finding.
    - *Is there money for maintenance — hardware replacement, the internet bill, an IT post?* Not
      "we will find it". A line, a source, an amount.
    - *Will you tell the staff yourself that this is how the hospital will work from now on?* If the
      director expects you to do the telling, you do not have a mandate.
3. **Ask what already exists.** There is almost always an existing finance package, and it is older
   than you expect. At Lunsar it was Quickbooks Enterprise 2016, and the administrator had asked for
   compatibility with it in 2018. Any previous digital attempt? Who ran it? What happened to it?
4. **Draft the agreement** — see below — and get it signed before you configure anything. It does not
   need lawyers. It needs names.
5. **Ask the director to announce it.** A staff meeting, a memo on the notice board, a word to each
   head of department. Something the nurse on Thursday has heard before you walk onto her ward.

## The agreement

Short. Specific. Names, not roles. It should say:

- **The hospital owns the system.** In those words.
- **The hospital owns the data.** And how patient consent is handled — in a setting with no national
  identity documents this is not a formality.
- **Training:** how many staff, released for how long, on which dates.
- **Remote support:** what your organisation will do after leaving, for how long, and what it will
  *not* do. Be honest — "the volunteers will answer WhatsApp for six months" is a commitment; write
  it down so that it ends.
- **Who pays for what after you go.** Hosting. Hardware replacement. The internet bill. The IT post.
  This is the clause everyone leaves out, and it is the one that predicts whether the system is alive
  in three years.

A template is in [B-3](../4-Instruments/B3-Mandate.md).

## What goes wrong

**The enthusiastic administrator.** One person at the hospital is delighted, capable, and makes
everything easy — and is not the director, and has no authority over clinical staff. You will build
the project around them because they are available. Then they are transferred. Build around the
*role*, not the person, and get the director's signature even when the administrator says it is not
necessary.

**The yes that means "we will not stop you".** Distinguish *"you may install it"* from *"we will use
it"*. Only the second is a mandate. The test is whether the director will say the second sentence to
staff in your presence.

**Agreeing with the wrong institution.** At Lunsar the only formal evaluation in the archive comes
from the academic partner in Barcelona, not the hospital. Get the hospital's own words on paper.

!!! warning "From the archive"

    At Yassa–Douala the board had prioritised digitalisation before the team arrived: rooms were
    released, staff time was allocated, the director and the IT technician championed it personally.
