# Retiring paper

**Produces:** one process that runs digital-only from a fixed, announced date — and the paper register
for that process, in a cupboard.
**When:** planned in week one; executed on a date the director sets, which may be after you leave.
**Instrument:** [D-4](../4-Instruments/D4-Exit-Gates.md); cutover plan in [L-1](../4-Instruments/L1-Handover-Pack.md).

## The rule

> The unit of migration is the **process**, not the institution. A process moves to digital only when
> the digital path can become the **only** path.

If you cannot say, for a specific process, on a specific date, *"the paper register is closed"* —
you are not ready to move that process. You are ready to work on the reason you cannot.

Anything short of this produces the failure described in
[*"They're doing both now"*](../2-Story/2.08-Doing-Both.md): two systems, doubled work, the slower
one skipped on busy days, the digital record incomplete, then untrusted, then dark. No decision is
ever taken. Nobody switches anything off.

## Choose the process, not the department

Pick the narrowest thing you can genuinely finish. Registration is almost always right:

- One desk, two or three staff, one well-defined event
- It is where identity is created, so everything later depends on it
- The digital path can plausibly become the only path within one deployment

"The wards" is almost always wrong: twenty staff across three shifts, several buildings, half a dozen
forms, and no moment at which the change completes.

## Preconditions — every one, for the specific process

- [ ] **It works when the power is out.** The device has a battery, or the process can wait an hour,
  or there is an agreed paper fallback *with a defined route back into the system* — which is not
  the same as a parallel register.
- [ ] **Everyone on every shift can do it.** Including nights. Including the person on leave this
  week who comes back to a changed process.
- [ ] **It is not slower than what it replaces.** Time it. Both ways. With the real person.
- [ ] **Someone at the hospital can fix the common failures** without calling Barcelona.
- [ ] **The director has set the date and announced it.** Not you. The hospital retires its own
  register.

## Steps

1. **Name the process and the register.** *"Outpatient registration, the blue book at the front desk."*
2. **Time the current process.** Ten real patients, stopwatch. Then time the digital path with the
   same clerk. If digital is slower, stop here and go to
   [simplification](../3B-Technical-Guide/Bahmni.md) — do not proceed on the promise that it will
   get faster.
3. **Run both for a bounded period — one to two weeks, with an end date.** This is the only
   legitimate parallel run: short, scheduled, and explicitly a test. Its purpose is to find the case
   the digital path cannot handle, not to let staff choose.
4. **Fix what the test found.**
5. **Set the date.** The director announces it, a week ahead, to the unit. The announcement says what
   happens on the day and what to do if the system is down.
6. **On the day: the book goes in a cupboard.** Not in the drawer. Not "just in case". Somewhere it
   takes effort to retrieve.
7. **For the first fortnight, someone is reachable at every shift change.** If you have flown home,
   that someone is the super-user, and they know how to reach tier 2.

## If you are leaving before the date

Do not rush a cutover in your last five days. A cutover with nobody around for the first fortnight is
worse than an honest handover that says: *"Phase 1 is configured and trained. The cutover is set for
six weeks after our departure. Here is exactly what happens on the day, who announces it, and what to
do when the system is down."*

Write that plan into the [handover pack](../4-Instruments/L1-Handover-Pack.md). Give the named owner
the authority and the checklist to run it.

## What goes wrong

**"Let them use whichever they prefer."** They will prefer the one that worked on Tuesday when the
power went. You have designed the failure.

**Retiring the register before the fallback exists.** Power goes, nobody can register a patient, the
clerk finds the book in the drawer, and it never goes back in the cupboard. Fallback first.

**Announcing it yourself.** A visiting engineer saying "from Monday, no more paper" is a suggestion. A
director saying it is an instruction.
