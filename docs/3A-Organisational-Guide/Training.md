# Training by role

**Produces:** seven different sessions for seven different jobs — and a super-user who can run them
again without you.
**When:** throughout the on-site period, not in one block at the end.

## Start by conceding the point

Training was delivered at Lunsar in 2024 — to technical and management staff, documented, with a user
manual, certified by a partner institution. Adoption did not follow.

So: **training is necessary and it is not sufficient.** If the next team says "the problem last time
was training", they are wrong, and this handbook has the certificate to prove it. What was missing is
in the other seven recipes of this part. This one is about doing the necessary part well.

## The mistake everyone makes

One all-staff session, in a room, with a projector. It serves nobody: the clerk needs one workflow
fast, the director needs no software at all, and the super-user needs three days.

Different people, different sessions, different places.

| Role | What they need | Where and how long |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration clerk** | One workflow, fast and correct; finding an existing patient by folder ID | At the desk, 30 min, repeated three times over a week |
| **Nurse** | Recording observations on a round; what to do when the system is down | On the ward, during a real shift, 45 min |
| **Pharmacist** | Dispensing and stock movement in the two screens that matter | At the counter, 45 min |
| **Laboratory technician** | Receive an order, enter a result, release it | In the lab, 45 min |
| **Medical officer** | Find a history, order, prescribe; the reports they may see | 30 min, their time is expensive |
| **Matron / administrator** | Produce the monthly return from the system, unaided | Whole session, timed to the month end |
| **IT officer / super-user** | Everything above, plus restart, users, backup **and restore**, escalation | Days, spread across the trip |
| **Director / board** | What it does, what it costs, what happens if nobody maintains it | One meeting, no software |

## Steps

1. **Train at the point of use**, on the real screen, with real (anonymised) patients. Never in a
   classroom.
2. **Short and repeated** beats long and once. Three thirty-minute sessions across a week, with the
   person doing real work in between, beats a three-hour session on Tuesday.
3. **The super-user trains the second cohort**, with you watching. If they cannot, you are not
   finished.
4. **Leave materials that survive without you:** a one-page task card per role, laminated, at the
   workstation; the in-product help ([tier 0](Support-Ladder.md)); short screen recordings if you can
   make them.
5. **Test comprehension by watching, not asking.** "Do you understand?" always gets yes. "Show me how
   you would register her" gets the truth.
6. **Train for failure as well as use.** Every session ends with: *the power has just gone — what do
   you do?*

## What goes wrong

**Training the people who came.** The ones who came are the ones with time. The night nurse in charge
did not come. Go to her.

**Training before simplifying.** If the screen still has twelve fields nobody uses, you are training
people to skip fields. Simplify first ([Bahmni](../3B-Technical-Guide/Bahmni.md)), then train.

**The certificate.** A certified training session is an event. Adoption is a Tuesday six months later.
Measure the Tuesday ([D-4](../4-Instruments/D4-Exit-Gates.md)).
