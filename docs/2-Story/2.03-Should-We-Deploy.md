# *"Should we be deploying this at all?"*

This is the chapter most handbooks do not have, and it is the one that will save you the most.

You have flights booked. Somebody has bought a server. There is a project title, a budget line, and a
report that will have to say what you achieved. Every incentive you have points toward installing
something.

Ask the question anyway.

## The counterexample is us

In February and March 2026, an AUCOOP team deployed Bahmni — a full hospital information system,
integrating clinical records, laboratory and pharmacy — at St. John of God Hospital in Lunsar.

At the time of that deployment the hospital had no full-time paid IT post, no funded owner for the
system after the team's departure, and no support contract with anyone. By the criteria in this
handbook's own [level decision](../4-Instruments/B2-Level-Decision.md), it qualified for level one or
two.

Four months later the hospital was producing every statutory return by hand.

That is not a story about a bad team. The engineering was good, the configuration was careful, the
training happened. It is a story about a system deployed several levels above what the institution
could carry — and about there being no instrument, at the time, that made that visible before the
decision was made.

This chapter is that instrument.

## The tool spectrum

There are four levels of digital health tool, and they are not a ladder you are obliged to climb.

| Level | What it does | Typical tools | What it takes to keep alive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Structured data collection and surveys | KoBoToolbox, ODK | A trained clerk. No server, on a hosted instance. |
| **2** | Aggregate reporting and case tracking | DHIS2, CommCare | A reporting officer. Alignment with the national system. |
| **3** | Clinical records at the point of care | OpenMRS | A part-time IT person. Reliable power where clinicians work. |
| **4** | Full hospital: clinical, lab, pharmacy, billing | Bahmni, GNU Health | A paid full-time IT person, a funded owner, and a support arrangement. |

Need always points at level 4. Every hospital would benefit from an integrated system. That is not
the question.

The question is **what the hospital can carry after you leave**, and that is a completely different
quantity — one that has nothing to do with how much the staff want it or how competent your team is.

!!! danger "Stop"

    Drop a level, or do not deploy, if **any** of these is true:

    - **No named person is paid to keep it running after you go.** Not "the IT technician will
      probably look after it". A name, a role, and a salary line that exists.
    - **The medical director has not personally endorsed it.** An administrator's enthusiasm is not
      the same authority.
    - **There is no route to fund maintenance for five years.** Hardware fails, software needs
      patching, someone has to be paid to do it.
    - **The unit you intend to digitise has no working power or network.** Verify this yourself, in
      the room, with a device.
    - **You cannot retire the paper process.** If paper continues alongside, you have not digitised
      anything — you have added a second job. See [chapter 8](2.08-Doing-Both.md).

## Doing less is not failing

The instinct that dropping a level is a defeat is the single most expensive instinct in this work.

A form-collection deployment that a ward uses every day for three years has changed how that hospital
works. A full hospital information system that is quietly abandoned four months after handover has
changed nothing, has consumed a year of volunteer effort, and has made the *next* team's job harder —
because the staff now have experience of a digital system that wasted their time.

**The failure is not doing too little. It is starting something the hospital cannot finish.**

## When the answer is no

Sometimes the honest conclusion is that this hospital is not ready for any of it this year. That is a
legitimate project outcome, and you should be prepared to write it in a report.

If you reach it, the trip is still worth taking, and there is a great deal of useful work in it:

- Complete the [assessment](../4-Instruments/B1-Readiness.md) properly and write it up. The next team
  inherits a real baseline instead of starting cold.
- Complete [data capture](../4-Instruments/D1-Data-Capture.md) — photograph the forms, map the
  workflow, measure the power and the network. This work does not expire.
- Fix the preconditions. Get power to the unit. Get the network into Emergency. Help the hospital
  make the case for an IT post. These are the things that make next year's answer *yes*.
- Deploy something at a level that fits, and do it properly.

That is a good project. It is a better project than a level-4 deployment that dies in the dry season.
