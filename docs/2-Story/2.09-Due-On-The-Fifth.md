# *"The return is due on the 5th"*

The matron finds you on the second-to-last Friday of the month, holding a stack of registers, and
asks whether the system can give her the malaria numbers.

She has three days to compile the monthly return. The way she does it now is to read back through
every ward register, counting, on paper, and then copy the totals onto the Ministry form. It takes
her most of two working days, every month, forever.

**This is the single best opportunity you will get, and most teams miss it because it does not look
like clinical work.**

## Why reporting is your lever

Everything else you have deployed asks staff to change how they work in exchange for a benefit that
arrives later and belongs partly to someone else. Reporting is different. It takes work *away* from
a specific named person, this month, for a deadline that already exists.

There is a second reason, and it is structural. The monthly return is the one thing in the hospital
that:

- happens on a fixed date, forever,
- has consequences if it is late,
- and requires data that was already entered for other reasons.

That makes it the natural place to put your system on the critical path. A system that produces
something the hospital is obliged to produce does not get quietly abandoned, because its absence is
noticed on the 5th of the following month.

## What you are actually being asked for

Be careful here, because "can it do reports" hides two very different requests.

**Clinical reports** — what a doctor wants to see about patients. Useful, and a nice demo.

**Management and statutory returns** — the aggregate counts the hospital must submit: bed state,
morbidity by diagnosis, deliveries, immunisations given, mortality, theatre cases. These are what the
matron is asking for, they are what the hospital is judged on, and they are what determines whether
your system is load-bearing or decorative.

The two audiences also need to be separated technically. A ward nurse should not see the board's
financial summaries; the board does not need the clinical listings. Whatever platform you use, gate
the reports by role — and check that the gating actually works before you rely on it, because
"privilege-based report filtering" is one of those features that is documented more reliably than it
is implemented.

!!! warning "From the archive"

    At Lunsar a reports tab was configured for phase 1 and gated on a management privilege — with
    the result that doctors could not open reports at all. It was found and fixed later.

    Test every role by logging in as that role. Not as an administrator with everything enabled.

## Do not build the national integration this trip

There is a real temptation, once you can produce the return, to go further: push the numbers directly
into the national health information system.

Do not, on a first deployment. Here is why.

Most countries run **DHIS2** nationally for aggregate health reporting, and Sierra Leone is one of
them. The Ministry launched a national health information hub in January 2025, and has stated that a
nationwide electronic medical record rollout follows. There is also, separately, an EMR pilot running
at a Freetown hospital with a private vendor, and an international agency tender for an
OpenMRS-family deployment at another.

In other words: the national picture is moving, and it is moving without reference to your project.

The right posture is to **produce the inputs cleanly and stay compatible** — use standard diagnosis
coding, aggregate by the administrative district the returns are reported by, and keep the export
simple enough that whoever does the national integration in two years can do it. Building a bespoke
integration now against a moving target is how you produce something that has to be thrown away.

!!! danger "Stop"

    Before designing any report, get a **blank copy and a completed copy** of the actual return, and
    find out who receives it and by when.

    A report built from a description of a form, rather than from the form, will be wrong in a way
    nobody notices until the month it matters.

## What "done" looks like

The exit gate for the reporting phase is not "reports exist". It is:

> **A member of hospital staff produces the statutory monthly return from the system, unaided, and
> submits it.**

If you have to be in the room, it is not done. If the matron still cross-checks it against the
registers by hand, it is not done either — although expect her to, for the first two or three months,
and expect that to be sensible rather than a lack of trust.
