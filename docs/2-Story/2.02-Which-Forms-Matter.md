# *"Which of these forms actually matters?"*

By day four you have a pile. Somebody has given you photocopies, somebody else has shown you a
cupboard, and a nurse has produced a bound register from under a desk that turns out to be the only
record of a whole ward's admissions.

You have Morbidity, and also IPD Morbidity, which is a different sheet. Bed State. Monthly Returns
Mortality, Monthly Returns Surgical, Monthly Returns X-Ray — three separate sheets with almost
identical headers. Emergency Department. Theatre. Dressing Cases. Two laboratory sheets. ANC. Safe
Motherhood. Immunization Returns.

Fourteen, and you are fairly sure that is not all of them.

The obvious question is which ones to digitise. It is the wrong question, and asking it first is how
teams end up building a beautiful form that nobody was required to fill in.

## The right question: which of these is the hospital *obliged* to produce?

There are two completely different kinds of paper in that pile, and confusing them will cost you a
week.

**Working records** are what staff write as they work: the ward register, the consultation note, the
laboratory day-book. They exist because the hospital needs them to function. Nobody outside the
building ever sees them.

**Statutory returns** are what the hospital must submit — monthly, to the Ministry of Health and
Sanitation, and quarterly to the church health association it belongs to. They are aggregate counts:
how many deliveries, how many malaria cases, how many beds occupied. They are compiled by hand at the
end of the month by someone reading back through the working records and counting.

Both matter. But they matter to *different people*, and that difference is your lever.

## Why the returns are your lever

A clinician's incentive to use your system is abstract — better care, eventually, for patients in
general. That is a real motivation and it is a weak one at 4 p.m. with forty people still waiting.

The person who compiles the monthly return has a different situation. They spend two or three days
every month reading back through registers and counting cases by hand, under a deadline, to produce
numbers that the hospital's funding and reporting depend on. If your system can produce that return —
correctly, from data that was going to be entered anyway — you have not given them a nice-to-have.
You have given them their week back.

**Find that person in your first three days.** They are usually a matron, a records officer, or the
administrator. They will become the most useful ally you have, and their monthly deadline is a real
recurring event you can attach your system to.

!!! warning "From the archive"

    In July 2026 — four months after a Bahmni deployment at Lunsar — every one of those fourteen
    returns was still being completed by hand.

    One of them, the Safe Motherhood report, is a photocopy of a form printed for February 2021, with
    "July 2026" written over the top in pen. That sheet has been photocopied from a photocopy for
    five years. It is the clearest single picture of what you are actually replacing.

## Photograph everything, now

You will not get a second chance. The team that comes after you will have questions you cannot
anticipate, and the hospital will not answer email reliably — not from unwillingness but because
people there are busy in a way that makes a request from Barcelona genuinely unreasonable.

So while you are standing in the room:

- One clean photograph of every **blank** form, and one of a **completed** example.
- The name of the unit it belongs to, and who fills it in.
- Where it goes next — who collects it, who signs it, what it feeds into.
- How many are filled per day or per month.

This is instrument [D-1](../4-Instruments/D1-Data-Capture.md), and it is the single most valuable
thing you will do that does not look like work.

!!! danger "Stop"

    If you cannot say, for a given form, *who is required to receive it and by when* — do not build
    anything that produces it yet. You are guessing at a requirement, and a guessed requirement
    becomes a module nobody uses.

## What good looks like at the end of this

You should be able to draw, on one page, the chain from *a patient walks in* to *a number appears in
a monthly return*. Every hop, every hand it passes through, every place it is copied from one book
into another.

Every copy step in that chain is a place where errors enter and time is lost — and it is also,
precisely, where a digital system earns its keep. Not by replacing the writing, but by removing the
copying.
