# *"It works here but not in Emergency"*

The registration desk is working. You have a laptop on the network, the server responds, a test
patient goes in and comes back out. You are, briefly, delighted.

Then you walk two hundred metres across the compound to Emergency, open the laptop, and there is
nothing. No signal at all.

Emergency is one of the units scheduled for phase one. It is also one of the units that feeds the
monthly bed-state return. And the network does not reach it.

## The map is not the site

Somewhere there is a diagram showing access points across the campus. It was drawn by a previous
team, it is a good diagram, and it describes what was *planned*.

At Lunsar, the campus network is a mesh of around twenty-eight access points — inexpensive routers
running OpenWrt, linked over 802.11s, with the site's uplink coming in over satellite. It is a
genuinely clever piece of work, built by people who knew what they were doing on a budget that did
not allow for anything else.

Whether every unit on the diagram is actually connected is a different question, and it is one the
diagram cannot answer.

!!! warning "From the archive"

    A network survey records that lightning protection groundings were installed at Emergency, and
    that the cable to it apparently was not. Whether it was ever run afterwards is not recorded
    anywhere in eight years of project files. Nobody wrote it down, and by the time anyone needed to
    know, there was no one to ask.

    That is the actual finding. Not "Emergency has no network" — *we do not know, and we cannot find
    out from here.*

**Verify coverage yourself, in the room, with a device, before you scope a phase around a unit.** It
takes an afternoon to walk the whole site. Not doing it is how you discover in week three that a
third of your plan is undeliverable.

## Latency is a real constraint, not a detail

A second thing the diagram will not tell you: how *slow* the link is at the far end of a mesh.

Each hop adds delay. At the far end of a chain, round-trip times can exceed a second. Whether that
matters depends entirely on what you are running over it:

- A **form-collection app** batches up entries and uploads them periodically. A second of latency is
  irrelevant.
- A **browser-based hospital information system** makes many small requests to render one screen. A
  second of latency per request turns "open the patient's record" into something a clinician will not
  wait for twice.

This distinction matters because the network at Lunsar was measured, and found acceptable, against a
form-collection tool. It has never been re-measured with a hospital information system running.

!!! danger "Stop"

    Measure latency **from the unit you intend to deploy in**, with the actual application, before
    committing a phase to that unit. Not from the server room. Not with a ping to the router.

    "The network is fine" is a claim about a place and an application, not about a campus.

## Cabling in a hospital compound

Some things about a hospital site that are not obvious from a network diagram:

- **Distances are larger than they look.** Wards, theatre, laboratory and administration are often
  separate single-storey buildings with open ground between them.
- **Lightning is a real design input.** At Lunsar the road crossing between two buildings is fibre,
  chosen specifically because glass carries no conductor and so cannot carry a strike from one
  building into another. That is a good decision and worth copying.
- **Walls are thick.** Concrete and rendered block will kill a signal that worked fine in the
  corridor.
- **The environment is hostile in ways your equipment was not designed for** — dust, humidity,
  insects, and the occasional rodent with an interest in cable insulation.

## Coverage is a phase-one requirement, not a nicety

The sequencing point: a unit without a verified working link cannot be in your first phase, no matter
how important it is clinically. If Emergency matters — and it does — then getting a link into
Emergency *is the project* for this trip, and the software goes in next time.

That is not a lesser outcome. It is the precondition, and someone has to do it.
