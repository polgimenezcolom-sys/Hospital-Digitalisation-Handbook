# *"The training went fine. Nobody used it after we left."*

The training was good. You know it was good — people came, they were engaged, they asked sensible
questions, they did the exercises. You have photographs. Somebody from the partner institution
certified that it happened.

Six months later, usage is close to zero.

## Training is necessary. It is not sufficient.

This is the hardest thing in this handbook to accept, because training is the intervention that feels
most obviously right, and because a team that trained well did nothing wrong.

But the evidence from this project is unusually clean on the point. In early 2024 a partner
institution formally certified that maintenance training had been delivered at Lunsar — to technical
*and* management staff, documented, with a user manual. Adoption did not follow.

So when the next team says "the problem last time was training", they will be wrong. Training was
delivered, documented and certified, and the system still went dark. **Whatever the missing
ingredient is, it is not more training.**

## What was actually missing

Three things, and they are all about what exists on an ordinary Tuesday six months later.

**Nobody was accountable.** No person at the hospital had "this system works" in their job. When it
broke, it was nobody's failure. When it went unused, nobody was answerable. A system that belongs to
everyone belongs to no one.

**There was no route for help.** A nurse hits a problem she cannot solve. What does she do? If the
honest answer is "message a volunteer in Spain and hope", then the system only works while that
volunteer is interested.

**Nothing depended on it.** Nothing broke when it was not used. No report failed, no drug went
unissued, no deadline was missed. A system nothing depends on is optional, and optional things lose
to busy days.

## The support ladder

Design the answer to *"what does someone do when they get stuck"* before you leave, with three tiers
and a real name at each one.

**Tier 0 — self-help, in the product.** Short task-focused help pages, in the system itself, reachable
from where the person is working. Not a PDF on a shared drive. It must work offline, because the
moment they need it is often the moment the connection is bad.

**Tier 1 — the super-user, on site.** One or two named people who understand the system well enough
to answer nine questions out of ten. They must be permanent staff, respected by their colleagues, and
— this is the part that gets skipped — **given the time**. A super-user who is also a full-time nurse
with no allowance for the role will help until the ward gets busy.

**Tier 2 — outside help.** The volunteer organisation, or better, a paid support arrangement with a
company that does this professionally. There are firms that support these platforms commercially in
the region. That costs money, and being able to name the cost is more useful to the hospital than
pretending it is free.

Write the ladder down, with names and contact routes, in the
[handover pack](../4-Instruments/L1-Handover-Pack.md).

## Put the system on the critical path

The strongest thing you can do for adoption is not motivational. It is structural: **make something
the hospital already cares about depend on the system.**

The monthly return ([chapter 9](2.09-Due-On-The-Fifth.md)) is the best candidate, because it has an
external deadline and consequences. Others: a drug cannot be dispensed without a record; a bill
cannot be closed; the bed-state figure the director quotes at the Monday meeting comes from the
system.

Once something real breaks when the system is not used, it stops being optional.

## Measure it, or you will not know

You will not be there, so decide now what you will look at and when. Four numbers, checked at six and
twelve months, are enough:

| | What you check |
|---|---|
| **Adoption** | How many of the staff who should be using it, are — this week |
| **Fidelity** | Are records entered at the point of care, or batched later from paper? |
| **Penetration** | How many units are live, out of those in scope |
| **Sustainability** | Is it still running at all |

For scale: a review in January 2024 found 2 of 11 digital forms carrying any data, with 568
submissions in total, all made within two months of the training. That is the baseline to beat, and
it is a low bar that has not yet been cleared.

!!! danger "Stop"

    If nobody has agreed to look at those numbers at six months, they will not be looked at, and you
    will never find out whether any of this worked.

    Put a name and a date in the handover pack. It can be your name.
