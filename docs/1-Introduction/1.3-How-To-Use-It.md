# How to use it

The handbook has five parts, and they answer different questions.

| Part | Answers | When you read it |
|---|---|---|
| **[The Story](../2-Story/index.md)** | *Why does this go wrong, and what actually causes it?* | Before you travel. Front to back. |
| **[The Organisational Guide](../3A-Organisational-Guide/index.md)** | *How do I do the non-technical 80%?* | Before and during. In order — each recipe feeds the next. |
| **[The Technical Guide](../3B-Technical-Guide/index.md)** | *How do I build and configure it?* | On site, one recipe at a time. |
| **[The Instruments](../4-Instruments/index.md)** | *What do I have to produce?* | Before, during and on the last day. |
| **[Real Deployments](../5-Real-Deployments/index.md)** | *What actually happened when someone tried?* | Whenever you want to know if an idea survives contact. |

The story is the part people skip and should not. It is roughly an hour to read and it exists because
every failure this project has recorded was avoidable by someone who understood the problem before
they started solving it.

## The trip is the structure

The instruments are ordered by when you use them, not by subject, because that is how you will
experience the work:

``` mermaid
graph LR
  A[Before you go<br/>B-1 · B-2 · B-3] --> B[While you are there<br/>D-1 · D-2 · D-3 · D-4]
  B --> C[Before you leave<br/>L-1]
  C --> D[Once you are back<br/>A-1]
  D --> E[6 and 12 months later<br/>S-1]
  E -.improves.-> A
```

Everything before the flight is about deciding *whether* and *what*. Everything on site is about
recording and building. The last day is about what stays behind.

Then there is a gap of six months, and **[S-1](../4-Instruments/S1-Follow-Up.md) is what happens in
it.** It is the only instrument completed after everyone has gone home, and the only one that records
an outcome rather than an intervention — whether the thing you built was still running, still used,
and still owned. Without it the loop is a line: nine forms describing what a team did, and nothing
about what became of it.

## The one rule

!!! danger "Record which version you followed"

    Write the handbook version into your closure report. It takes five seconds.

    Without it nobody can ever connect an outcome to a version of this document, which means the
    handbook can never be shown to be working or failing — and a guide that cannot be tested is just
    an opinion that got typed up.

    This handbook is **v0.4**.

## Two conventions

**Boxes mean something.**

!!! danger "Stop"
    A condition under which the right decision is to do less, or nothing. Ignoring these is how
    projects end up worse than if they had never started.

!!! warning "From the archive"
    Something this actually happened to. Usually to us.

!!! tip "Guide reference"
    A pointer to the step-by-step version of what has just been explained.

**Instruments have codes.** `B-1`, `D-3`, `L-1`. They are short so that people say them out loud —
*"has anyone finished D-1 for the pharmacy?"* is a question a team can actually ask each other in a
corridor.

## If it is wrong, say so

This handbook was written from one deployment and a decade of other people’s project files, by people who got several things
wrong the first time. Where it does not match what you find, that is information — and it is the most
valuable thing your project can send back.

[How to contribute](../contributing/index.md) explains how, and it is deliberately as small a task as
we could make it.
