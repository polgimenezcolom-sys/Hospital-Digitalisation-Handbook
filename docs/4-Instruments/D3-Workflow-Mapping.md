# D-3 · Workflow mapping

**When:** first week, before configuring anything.
**Produces:** the patient journey as it actually is.

## The standard sequence

To be confirmed or corrected on site — it is a starting point, not an answer:

1. **Registration** — patient identified or registered, assigned to a queue
2. **Triage** — vital signs measured, urgency assessed
3. **Consultation** — symptoms recorded, diagnosis formulated
4. **Laboratory** — tests ordered, samples collected and processed, results recorded
5. **Prescription** — medication prescribed on diagnosis and results
6. **Pharmacy** — medication dispensed, stock decremented
7. **Billing** — services and medications invoiced
8. **Discharge or admission**

## For each step, record

- Current method — register, logbook, verbal
- Average daily volume
- Staff involved
- **The pain point the staff themselves name** — not the one you noticed

## The output

One page showing the chain from *a patient walks in* to *a number appears in a monthly return*. Every
hop, every hand it passes through, every place data is copied from one book into another.

**Every copy step is where errors enter and time is lost — and precisely where a digital system earns
its keep.** Not by replacing the writing, but by removing the copying.

!!! note "The design rule"

    The unit of migration is the **process**, not the institution. A process moves to digital only
    when the digital path can become the *only* path.
    ([Chapter 8](../2-Story/2.08-Doing-Both.md).)
