# S-1 · Six- and twelve-month check

**When:** at six months, and again at twelve, on the dates written into [L-1](L1-Handover-Pack.md).
**Produces:** the only outcome this handbook ever measures.

!!! danger "Why this instrument exists"

    Every other instrument here is completed between the day you decide to go and the fortnight after
    you land. But the thing this handbook is about — whether a deployment survives — happens over
    **years**, and it happens after everyone has gone home.

    Before v0.4 three separate forms deferred to a six- and twelve-month check ([D-4](D4-Exit-Gates.md)'s
    sustainability row, [A-1](A1-Debrief.md) section 5, [L-1](L1-Handover-Pack.md) section 4) and
    none of them was a document anyone could fill in. There was a name and a date and nowhere to put
    the answer. So the handbook recorded, in detail, everything that was *done* — and nothing about
    what became of it.

    That is not a small omission. Without an outcome, a stack of completed instruments can tell a
    future reader what five cohorts did and never which of it mattered.

## Ten minutes, remotely

This is deliberately the smallest instrument in the set. It is designed to be answerable from **one
conversation with the super-user**, by phone or messaging, because that is what will realistically
happen. If it takes an afternoon it will not be done.

You are not auditing anyone, and nothing here is a test the hospital can fail. A blank field is a
finding. An invented one is worse than nothing.

## What it asks

- [ ] **Is it running?** Reachable at all · units live against units handed over · who is actually
      using it · entered at the point of care or batched from paper afterwards
- [ ] **Paper.** Which processes were retired at handover, and are they still retired? If a register
      came back, when, and what triggered it
- [ ] **The owner.** Is the person named in L-1 still in post? Is the post funded? Do the super-users
      still have their time allowance?
- [ ] **What broke** since you left — including what broke and was *not* fixed
- [ ] **[B-1](B1-Readiness.md) re-scored** — the same seven dimensions, against the same anchors
- [ ] **The four outcomes**, with the same definitions used at [D-4](D4-Exit-Gates.md)
- [ ] **What the hospital wants that it is not getting** — the next deployment's requirements list,
      a year early and for free

## The re-score is the point

[B-1](B1-Readiness.md) is scored before you travel, again at departure, and again here. Scored once it
is a screening number. Scored three times it is a **measurement of whether the gap actually closed** —
and it is the only number in this handbook that can be compared between one deployment and another, or
between one hospital and another.

A dimension that has not moved in twelve months is the finding. Write down why nothing moved; that
sentence is worth more to the next team than the score.

!!! warning "Silent downtime is the failure mode this project keeps missing"

    In February 2026 a remote check found the Lunsar server inactive. It survives in the archive as
    one line in a Word file, with no recorded follow-up. Nobody knows what happened, or for how long,
    or whether anyone at the hospital noticed.

    Section 5 of this form exists because of that line. If something broke and stayed broken, that is
    the single most useful thing you can record.

## Whose job this is

**Not the deploying team's.** That is the whole design. Every other instrument is completed by people
who are on the trip; by month six they have graduated, changed cities, or moved on to another project.

S-1 belongs to the person named in [L-1](L1-Handover-Pack.md) section 4, with a date, agreed before
departure and written down while everyone is still in the room. It can be your own name. It cannot be
"someone will check".

!!! note "This works while the feedback owner is still unsettled"

    S-1's owner is named **per deployment**, in L-1 — not centrally. So it does not wait on the
    unresolved question of who merges the [A-1](A1-Debrief.md) returns
    ([see the open box there](A1-Debrief.md)). File the completed check with the deployment's other
    instruments, and send a copy to the handbook repository as an issue. If someone is eventually
    named to merge the returns, they inherit a queue that already exists.
