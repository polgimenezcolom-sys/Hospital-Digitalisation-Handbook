Operational handbook · not the thesis

Deploying a hospital information system, one trip at a time

What an AUCOOP team does before it travels, while it is on site, on the day it leaves, and after it gets home. Nine instruments, one stop rule, and a way of feeding what you learned back into the next edition.

Version 0.1 (structure draft) Owner Pol Giménez Colom Companion TFM, UPC ETSETB Reference site St. John of God, Lunsar (SL)

How to use this

Read the stage you are in. Nothing else.

Every other guide in this field — WHO's, the ministry's, the software vendor's — is organised by system lifecycle: assess, select, configure, deploy, evaluate. That is how an institution with permanent staff experiences the work.

You do not experience it that way. You experience a trip. So this handbook is ordered the way the trip runs, and each instrument tells you the one thing to produce at that moment. An instrument is done when it has been filled in — not when it has been read.

The one rule

Record which version of this handbook you followed, in the closure report. Without that, no outcome can ever be attributed to a version, and the next edition learns nothing from yours.

Cross-cuttingapplies at every stage

The stop rule

The decision this handbook exists to make possible is the decision not to deploy.

A well-configured Level 1 tool that staff actually use is worth more than a half-deployed hospital information system that is abandoned when you fly home. Deploying above what the hospital can sustain is not ambition; it is the documented way these projects fail.

Drop a level, or do not deploy, if any of the following is true at the end of B-1:

  • No named person at the hospital is paid to keep the system running after you leave. Not "someone will look after it". A name, a role, and a salary line.
  • The medical director has not personally endorsed the project. Endorsement from an administrator or a department head is not the same thing.
  • There is no route to fund maintenance for the next five years.
  • The unit you intend to digitise has no working power or network link. Verify it yourself, in the room. At Lunsar, Emergency was scheduled for Phase 1 and it is still unknown whether the network cable was ever run — the groundings went in, and nobody wrote down what happened next. That uncertainty is the finding.
  • You cannot retire the paper process. If paper and digital both continue, you have doubled the workload and created nothing.
Why this is first

Across six AUCOOP editions between 2018 and 2026, no deployment was ever switched off — because paper was never switched off either. The failure mode is not rejection. It is a second system running quietly beside the first until nobody bothers with it.

Stage 1 — Before you goweeks −8 to −1

Before you go

Three instruments. All of them can be completed remotely, and all of them should be, because on-site days are the scarcest resource you have.

B-1

Readiness assessment

needs design

Scores the gap between what the system will assume and what the hospital actually is. Produces a number that feeds the level decision in B-2.

Built on Heeks' design–reality gap model: rate each dimension 0–10 for the distance between the proposed system's assumptions and the hospital as found. Higher total, higher risk of failure.

DimensionWhat you are ratingScore
InformationWhat the system expects to be recorded vs. what is written down today0–10
TechnologyPower, network, hardware assumed vs. installed and working0–10
ProcessesThe workflow the software implies vs. the patient journey in D-30–10
Objectives & valuesWhat the deploying team wants vs. what the hospital wants0–10
Staffing & skillsDigital literacy assumed vs. present on the ward0–10
Management systemsGovernance and accountability assumed vs. actual0–10
Other resourcesMoney and time assumed vs. committed0–10

Then four questions the model does not cover, because they decide survival rather than fit:

PAID-IT
Is there a paid IT person at the hospital? Yes / No
OWNER-5Y
Is there a funded owner for the next five years? Yes / No
PRESENCE
How long is AUCOOP committed to this site? Years
HANDOVER
Will a handover pack (L-1) exist on departure? Yes / No
Open — Pol to settle

The score thresholds that map a total to a level in B-2 are not fixed yet. They should be calibrated against Lunsar and Douala, where the outcomes are known, before this ships.

B-2

Level decision

draft

Chooses how far up the tool spectrum this deployment goes. Driven by sustaining capacity, not by need — need always points at Level 4.

LevelScopeToolsMinimum to sustain it
1Data collection & surveysKoBoToolbox, ODKA trained clerk. No server, if you use a hosted instance.
2Health management & case trackingDHIS2, CommCareA reporting officer; alignment with the national HMIS.
3Clinical EMROpenMRSA part-time IT person; reliable power in the clinical areas.
4Full HIS — clinical, lab, pharmacy, billingBahmni, GNU HealthA paid full-time IT person, a funded five-year owner, and a support contract.

If B-1 does not support the level you wanted, drop one and execute it properly. That is the stop rule doing its job, not a defeat.

B-3

Mandate & ownership

draft

Secures the authority the deployment will need, in writing, before anybody flies.

People to identify and engage by name:

RoleWhy they matter
Medical DirectorMust formally endorse the project and communicate expectations to clinical staff directly.
Head of NursingNurses perform the highest volume of data entry; ward adoption runs through this role.
Head of PharmacyOwns stock management adoption.
Laboratory DirectorOwns the lab module and the result-delivery chain.
Administrative / Finance DirectorOwns billing configuration and financial reporting. Ask what finance system already exists.
IT OfficerBecomes the primary super-user and administrator. If this post does not exist, see the stop rule.

The MoU must cover: system ownership · data ownership and consent · training commitments and released staff time · remote support arrangements · who pays for what, after you leave.

Evidence

At Douala the hospital board prioritised digitalisation and released rooms, staff time and institutional backing. At Lunsar it did not. Douala is the site that adopted — and it is the clearest contrast the project has produced. Two caveats worth carrying: the board was not the only difference (Douala also had a named super-user pair, the IT technician and the director), and the comparison spans one year, not eight.

Stage 2 — While you are therethe on-site days

While you are there

Assume you will never be able to ask again. Every question you leave unanswered on site becomes a year of unanswered email.

D-1

Field data capture

core

The record you cannot reconstruct from Barcelona. Photograph, measure and write down everything on this list before you leave the building it is in.

Paper records — photograph every form in use

One clean, legible photograph of each blank form and one of a completed example, per unit. These define what the system must be able to produce.

GroupForms in use at Lunsar (July 2026 returns)
Statutory returnsMorbidity · IPD Morbidity · Bed State · Monthly Returns Mortality · Monthly Returns Surgical · Monthly Returns X-Ray
Clinical unitsEmergency Department · Theatre · Dressing Cases · Laboratory (2 sheets)
Maternal & childANC · Safe Motherhood · Immunization Returns

Fourteen sheets. Separately, from a July 2026 capture: two pages of the patient booklet and the folder-ID card it carries.

Why this instrument exists

Eight years of AUCOOP archive contain no photograph of a paper form of any kind. The fourteen statutory returns above were finally collected in September 2026, and the booklet pages in July — both retroactively, from Barcelona, months after anyone was last on site. A photograph of a ward register is still missing, and the hospital's bed capacity is still recorded as 25, 85 or 100 depending on which document you open.

What else to capture, per unit

  • Daily volume, counted not estimated OPD visits/day, inpatient census, lab tests/day, deliveries/month.
  • Who fills the form, who signs it, where it goes next
  • How the patient is identified at this point At Lunsar the folder ID minted at Reception is the join key for the entire paper system.
  • Power: measured voltage over 24 h, grid hours/day, generator runtime
  • Network: is there a working link in this room, and measured latency
  • Photograph of the room itself — outlets, cable routes, where a workstation could physically go

Deployment record

Written on site, not from memory afterwards: every access point with its location and model; switch and router models and where they sit; cable routes and distances; solar panel count, orientation and rating; UPS model and measured runtime; server specification and where it lives; every credential created, recorded for rotation, never in a shared document.

D-3

Workflow mapping

from the thesis

Document the patient journey as it is, before proposing to change it.

The standard sequence, to be confirmed or corrected on site:

  1. Registration — patient identified or registered, assigned to a queue
  2. Triage — vital signs measured, urgency assessed
  3. Consultation — symptoms recorded, diagnosis formulated
  4. Laboratory — tests ordered, samples collected and processed, results recorded
  5. Prescription — medication prescribed on diagnosis and results
  6. Pharmacy — medication dispensed, stock decremented
  7. Billing — services and medications invoiced
  8. Discharge or admission

For each step record: current method (register, logbook, verbal), average daily volume, staff involved, and the pain point the staff themselves name.

Design rule

The unit of migration is the process, not the institution. A process moves to digital only when the digital path can become the only path.

D-4

Deployment phases & exit gates

gates need design

What each phase contains, and what "done" means. A phase is not finished because the feature works.

PhaseContainsExit gate
1Registration & basic EMR — patient identity, encounters, clinical formsThe folder ID is the primary searchable identifier · a runbook exists · one unit has retired its paper register
2Laboratory & pharmacy — orders, results, dispensing, stockA lab order completes end to end · dispensing decrements stock · pharmacist can work unaided
3Billing & administrationReconciles with the incumbent finance system · the finance director signs off
4Reporting & epidemiologyThe statutory monthly return in D-1 can be produced from the system, by hospital staff, unaided

Every gate, at every phase, carries the same two conditions: the feature works offline-tolerantly, and a hospital-usable runbook exists — written for someone with no prior IT experience, on the assumption that a volunteer visits once or twice a year.

Open — Pol to settle

Gates are currently written as engineering conditions. They need the measurable outcome criteria from the Success measures section attached to each one, so a later team can test whether a gate was genuinely passed.

Stage 3 — Before you leavethe last two days

Before you leave

One instrument, and the archive says it is the one that decides whether any of this survives.

L-1

Handover pack

core

What physically stays at the hospital. Assembled on site and walked through with the named owner, in person, before you go.

  • Named owner, written down, with role and contact details
  • Runbook per deployed unit — how to do the daily task, with screenshots Written for zero prior IT experience. If it cannot be followed by someone who has never used a computer, it is not finished.
  • "What to do when it breaks" sheet — the five most likely failures and the first thing to try
  • Credentials handed over securely and recorded for rotation Never in a shared document, never in the runbook, never in an appendix.
  • Support escalation ladder — tier 0 self-help, tier 1 the local super-user, tier 2 remote or contracted support, each with a name and a route
  • Backup procedure, demonstrated once by the owner while you watch
  • What was deliberately not done, and why The next team's scope starts here. This is the single most useful page in the pack.
  • The version of this handbook you followed
Evidence

Six AUCOOP editions, 2018–2026. One left a handover pack — the 2024 cohort — and that edition was inherited and extended by the two that followed it. The deployments left without one did not fare the same way. KoboCollect (2023) carried data in 2 of 11 forms a year later. RosarioSIS (2018) cannot even be confirmed to have been used: the closure report records it installed and the administrator trained, the 2024 cohort recorded him saying it was only ever a plan, and nobody wrote down which is true.

Note what that means. These systems were not switched off — they went dark without anyone deciding to stop them, which is why the stop rule above is phrased the way it is.

Stage 4 — Once you are backstarted on site, closed within two weeks

Once you are back

This is the instrument that makes the handbook improve rather than age.

A-1

Debrief & feedback return

needs design

What the next edition needs to know, in a form it can actually act on.

Design constraints — these are not preferences:

  • It is part of the CCD closure report you already have to write. A new voluntary task will not be completed; an extension of a mandatory one will. Whether CCD's format can actually be extended is unconfirmed, and the whole loop depends on it.
  • Completed in the field, before you fly. Not afterwards. People land in Barcelona and stop.
  • Under two hours in total. A half-filled matrix is worse than none, because it looks like data.
  • One named person merges returns into the next version, on a fixed cadence.

What it asks:

  • Which handbook version did you follow?
  • Which instruments did you complete, and which did you skip? Why? A skipped instrument is a finding about the handbook, not a failure of the team. Say so plainly, or nobody will report it.
  • Where did the handbook say something that turned out to be wrong on site?
  • What did you have to invent because the handbook did not cover it?
  • Scores against the success measures below, at departure
  • The three questions you wish you had asked while you were still there
Open — Pol to settle

Who owns the merge, and how often? Without a named owner and a cadence, three versions of this handbook will exist within two years and none will be canonical. This has already happened once to the project's control document.

What success means, measurably

Without these, version 2 of this handbook has nothing to compare itself against.

OutcomeThe question it answersHow you would measure it here
AcceptabilityDo staff find it agreeable?Short structured question at handover
AdoptionDid they take it up at all?Proportion of eligible staff with an active account using it weekly
AppropriatenessDoes it fit this setting?Workflow steps matched vs. worked around
FeasibilityCan it actually be done here?Phases completed vs. planned
FidelityIs it used as intended?Records entered at the point of care vs. batched later from paper
Implementation costWhat did it really take?Hardware, travel, staff time, and the recurrent cost after departure
PenetrationHow far did it spread?Units live vs. units in scope
SustainabilityIs it still running?Still in use at 6 and 12 months, checked remotely

Framework: Proctor et al. (2011), implementation outcomes; the measurement column is our own operationalisation, not theirs. Baseline to beat, from Lunsar: a January 2024 review found 2 of 11 digital forms carrying any data at all, with 568 submissions in total, all of them made within two months of the training.

Say this before someone else does

By B-2's own criteria, Lunsar qualified for Level 1 or 2. It has no paid full-time IT post, no funded five-year owner and no support contract — and AUCOOP deployed Bahmni, a Level 4 system, there. Four months after the February–March 2026 trip the hospital was still producing every statutory return by hand.

That is not an argument against this handbook. It is the argument for it: the stop rule exists because the project has already walked past the point where it should have stopped, and had no instrument that made that visible at the time.

Technical annexes

The dimensioning and configuration detail. Rationale for every choice lives in the thesis; what follows is the part you use on site.

Power

Total IT load, then the UPS rating that covers it:

P_total = Σ (P_i × Q_i)  [W]
Example: 50 (server) + 250 (PoE switch + budget) + 60 (4 APs) + 400 (8 laptops) + 30 (NAS) = 790 W
S_UPS = (P_total × t_runtime) / (PF × η_UPS)  [VA]
Example: (790 × 0.5) / (0.8 × 0.87) = 568 VA → specify 1000–1500 VA for headroom
Ranges: t_runtime 0.25–1 h · PF ≈ 0.8 for IT loads · η_UPS 0.85–0.90

The UPS must include automatic voltage regulation. Grid voltage in these settings swings between roughly 170 V and 260 V against a 220–240 V nominal, and a power failure during a database write corrupts data.

Network

Dimensioning, core topology, WiFi coverage planning, VLAN segmentation, and a worked design for the model hospital. Measure mesh latency against the actual application: above one second is tolerable for periodic form uploads and likely unworkable for a browser-based EMR. At Lunsar the >1 s figure was measured against KoboCollect and has never been re-measured with the EMR running — do that before go-live.

The network layer is covered in far more depth by AUCOOP's own Community Network Handbook — mesh, addressing, OpenWrt, monitoring, VPN and power all have step-by-step recipes there. Use it rather than duplicating it.

Server

Hardware selection, operating system and virtualisation, redundancy, and backup and disaster recovery. Note that Bahmni's OpenMRS container takes 3–12 minutes to become healthy after a restart — do not assume a failed boot before that. One rebuild with a large search index has been observed to take around 26 minutes.

Remote access

Carrier-grade NAT blocks all inbound connections, which means no remote administration, no cloud backup and no updates without a workaround. The answer is a hub-and-spoke WireGuard tunnel via a VPS relay. Check for CGNAT during D-2 by comparing the router's WAN address against the public address.

Monitoring & maintenance

System health, UPS state, VPN tunnel liveness, and the preventive maintenance schedule the named owner follows.

Open — Pol to settle

These annexes currently point at the thesis. In v0.2 the procedures themselves move here and the thesis keeps only the rationale — that is the split the change plan describes, and it is the largest single piece of work remaining on this document.
